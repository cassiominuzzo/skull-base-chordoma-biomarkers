Criteria applied to all comparisons (Iorio et al., BMJ 2015; Foroutan et al., J Clin Epidemiol 2020; Guyatt et al., J Clin Epidemiol 2011; Santesso et al., J Clin Epidemiol 2020): (1) evidence from prognostic cohorts starts at high certainty; (2) risk of bias (QUIPS): −1 when most of the evidence is at high risk, −2 only when the limitation is likely to change the direction or magnitude of the effect, judged by comparing estimates with and without the limitation; (3) inconsistency: −1 for estimates in opposite directions with I² ≥50%, or when the prediction interval includes 1 while the confidence interval does not; no downgrading when estimates point in the same direction, heterogeneity is moderate and the confidence interval already includes 1; (4) imprecision: binary scale, −1 when the CI includes 1 or the total number of events is below the optimal information size (~190 events to detect HR 1.5 with 80% power and two-sided alpha 0.05, Schoenfeld formula); continuous scale, −1 when the CI of the HR per 5 points includes 1; −2 when the CI is very wide with k≤2 or fewer than ~50 events; (5) indirectness: −1 when platform, zygosity or outcome differ from the review question; (6) publication bias: tested formally only with k≥10 (funnel plot and Egger test); (7) subgroup differences with fewer than 3 studies in one level are described but not used to change certainty; (8) no upgrading when already downgraded for risk of bias.
